📋 Using AI Safely at Harwin — the basics

AI is powerful when used well and risky when it isn't. Three rules cover most of it:

  1. Use the approved accounts. Harwin has corporate versions of ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, and Claude with the right paperwork in place. Use those for any work data — never the free or personal versions.
  2. Strip out what the AI doesn't need. Names, addresses, photos, anything identifying — remove before upload. The model still does the job.
  3. A human reviews every consequential output. AI drafts, you decide. Especially anything that touches a customer, candidate, or external party.
When to pause and check first
  • First time you're using AI on someone's personal data — candidate, customer, supplier, employee
  • Anything sensitive: health, religion, ethnicity, trade union, sexual orientation
  • Anything where the AI would replace a human decision, not support one
A note on last month's CV Sifter: It was a starter pattern to show what's possible — not yet an approved Harwin tool. Before any team uses it on real CVs, there'll be the proper governance work in place.
🚨 7 Ways Agents Bite Back

Agents are powerful precisely because they act without you watching. That's also exactly why they need governance. Here's what goes wrong when they don't have it — with real-world cases.

1
Hallucinated decisions
Air Canada's chatbot invented a bereavement-fare refund policy that didn't exist. A passenger acted on it. The court made Air Canada honour the promise — and dismissed the airline's defence that the chatbot was a "separate legal entity". (Feb 2024.)
Fix: Always ask the agent for the source row/column reference, never the conclusion alone. Verify before acting.
2
GDPR / privacy violations
Samsung engineers pasted proprietary source code into ChatGPT to debug it. The data left the building. Samsung issued a global ban on consumer AI tools weeks later — too late to retrieve what was already out. (April 2023.)
Fix: Enterprise-tier accounts only. Free or personal accounts don't have DPAs — whatever you paste can train the next model.
3
Audit trail gone
Amazon's internal CV-screening AI penalised CVs that mentioned "women's" (as in "women's chess club captain"). It had learned the bias from a decade of male-dominated hiring data. Scrapped before deployment. (Reuters, 2018.)
Fix: Every agent-assisted decision needs a human reasoning step, recorded in writing, in a system you control.
4
Cost runaway
A test agent left running over a long weekend. A retry loop kicked in on a paid model. £4,200 in API charges before the credit-card alert fired on Monday morning.
Fix: Hard spend caps on every API key. Rate limits. Daily budget alerts to Finance.
5
Skill atrophy
Drivers reliant on satnav lose the ability to navigate routes they used to know by heart. The same pattern is showing up with AI summaries — teams read the summary, never the original, and miss what only the raw text shows.
Fix: Treat agents as second readers, not replacements for first reads. Rotate who does the unaided review.
6
Speed without judgement
Sports Illustrated published product reviews under fake AI-generated author profiles, complete with AI headshots and invented bios. Futurism exposed it in November 2023. The brand's credibility never fully recovered.
Fix: Drafts only; humans send. Especially anything with reputational or commercial weight.
7
Shadow AI
Samsung, Apple, Verizon, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs — all restricted consumer AI at work in 2023. Same pattern every time: employees pasting confidential material into free tools they had signed up for personally.
Fix: Clear written policy: personal accounts for personal stuff, corporate accounts for anything Harwin. Tell your team explicitly, more than once.
💬 The Quiet Power of Chatbots

Not everything needs to be an agent. A chatbot is just AI you talk to — one task, your hands on the wheel, output you read before acting on. Boring? Maybe. Safer? Almost always. Often, exactly the right tool.

Reach for a chatbot when…
  • The task is bounded and one-off — draft this email, summarise this report
  • You'll read the output before acting on it
  • You don't want anything happening when you're not watching
  • Nobody else needs to see the output
  • You're thinking, not automating — writing-as-thinking, pressure-testing a decision, exploring options
Real examples worth highlighting
📧 Drafting tricky emails
The GDPR reply you've been putting off? Pure chatbot work. You paraphrase the situation, ask for a measured draft, edit until it sounds like you, send. No scheduling, no auto-anything.
🔍 Pre-call research
Five minutes before a supplier call: "Brief me on Genalog's recent news, financial position, and what their competitors are doing." Read the briefing, walk in prepared. No scheduling needed.
🧠 "Is this a good idea?"
Pressure-test a decision. "I'm thinking of switching to weekly stand-ups. Argue for it; argue against it; what am I missing?" Free consulting, available at 11pm before the board pack is due.
📊 Code / formula review
Paste in a Power BI DAX measure or an Excel formula. Ask Claude or ChatGPT to explain it, suggest improvements, spot edge cases. You stay the engineer of record.
📝 Notes → structure
Drop in your messy meeting notes. "Turn this into actions with owners and due dates, plus three open questions for the next meeting." Five minutes saved, every meeting.
🔤 Plain-English translation
Legal clause, supplier T&Cs, regulatory paragraph — "explain this to me like I'm a smart non-lawyer". You still verify with legal for anything that matters, but you arrive at the conversation already half-up-to-speed.
🎯 Agent or Chatbot? A Decision Card

Same AI underneath. Very different risk profile depending on how you deploy it. Use this card when you're tempted to "automate" something.

Question If yes →
Does it need to repeat without you (overnight, weekend, every Monday at 8am)?Agent — with kill switch + audit
Will the output trigger a real action (send email, change record, write to a system)?Agent — with human approval step
Could a wrong answer cause real harm (financial / regulatory / reputational / candidate fairness)?Don't deploy without a proper review first
Will you read every output before acting?Chatbot — you're already in the loop
Is this one-off thinking — exploring, drafting, summarising, brainstorming?Chatbot — agent would be overkill
The honest default: if you're not sure, start with a chatbot. Once you've used it manually 10+ times and you're tired of the repetition, that's the signal to graduate to an agent — with the governance in place this time.
💡 Tips & Tricks (updated for June)
Tip #1: Always have a kill switch. Write into every agent prompt: "If you receive output >5MB, hit an error condition, or process more than 50 items in a run — stop and notify [you]." Otherwise schedules fail silently for weeks.
Tip #2: Review schedules monthly. Open the Power Automate run history. Anything not opened or read by anyone in 30 days? Kill the schedule. Agents nobody reads are noise tax.
Tip #3: No personal Claude Pro / ChatGPT Plus for work data. They don't have the right DPA. Corporate accounts only. (Yes, even just for "a quick thing".)
Tip #4: Hard spend caps on every API key. Anthropic and OpenAI both let you set a monthly cap in the console. Set it conservatively — a runaway loop can burn £1,000 overnight.
Tip #5: Drafts only, humans send. Anything that touches a customer, candidate, or external party — agent produces a draft, a human reviews, a human sends. Always.
Tip #6: Redact before upload. Strip names, addresses, ID numbers, anything that could reveal a protected characteristic. The model doesn't need it; you don't need the risk.
Tip #7: Record the reasoning, not just the output. When a human accepts an agent's recommendation, they write a sentence saying why. That sentence is your audit trail. Without it, you have AI output you can't defend.
