📰 The month in AI

Four stories worth knowing about — one close to home, one about the tools we use, one about our customers, and one reality check.

🗄️
The close-to-home one: AI that lives inside the database
Earlier this year, Oracle launched a tool that lets companies build AI assistants that sit inside their Oracle database. Instead of exporting data out to an AI, the AI comes to the data — and the database's own security rules still apply, so people only see what they're already allowed to see.

Why it's worth knowing: IFS runs on Oracle, so this is the kind of technology that could one day let you type "Which orders slipped past their promised date last month?" and get the answer straight from our own system. It's a sign of where things are heading: AI that comes to the data, not data copied out to AI.
Source: Oracle announcement (24 March 2026), Oracle blog.
🧠
The tools one: a new top Claude model, for less
On 22 September Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, now its most capable model — and it's available on Team plans like ours. It handles very long documents in one go, such as a complete quality manual or a lengthy email thread. Anthropic says it costs around 40% less to run on typical workloads than Opus 5.

More capable doesn't mean you should use it for everything — see Tip 2 below.
Source: Anthropic (22 September 2026).
🛒
The customer one: when the buyer is an AI
Amazon now lets its sellers manage listings, stock and sales data from inside AI assistants like Claude, rather than working only in Amazon's own seller tools. Amazon says 90% of its selling partners already use third-party AI tools to run their businesses.

The same shift is coming to our industry. Buyers at distributors like DigiKey, Avnet and TTI will increasingly use AI to search, compare and shortlist parts. The question for Harwin: can an AI easily read and understand our product information?
Source: Amazon (23 September 2026).
✈️
The reality check: AI in defence manufacturing
Many of our customers work in aerospace and defence, so this one's worth watching. One market research report estimates AI-driven "software-defined factories" in US defence manufacturing could grow from about $150M to $1.8B a year by 2030, and Lockheed Martin has already backed two start-ups in the space.

The detail the headlines skipped: the same report notes industrial robots can be 2.5 to 11 times less stiff than traditional CNC machines, so they often need extra correction to hit precision tolerances.

What it means for us: our aerospace and defence customers are investing in how they build. Faster, more automated factories still need components that are right first time — with the quality records to prove it. That's the part of the story that plays to Harwin's strengths.
Source: GlobeNewswire (24 September 2026).
🧭 What this means for us

Our last issue's message was keep a human in the loop. This month adds a second idea that fits alongside it: the best AI goes to where the data already lives, rather than the data being copied out to wherever the AI is.

In practice, that means three things at Harwin:

  • Don't copy whole exports into a chat. Paste only what the task needs, in an approved tool. The bigger the dump, the bigger the risk.
  • Our product data is becoming a sales channel. Clear, consistent datasheets help human buyers today — and AI buyers tomorrow.
  • Slow, repetitive reports are the easiest win. That's where automation pays back fastest — which is why we're launching Report Rescue below.
Same rule as always: AI drafts, a person decides. Building or scheduling something that runs on its own? Loop in IT first (itsupport@harwin.com).

Four rules our data follows
1. One source of truth IFS
IFS is the master record for orders and invoices. Every bookings and billings figure can be traced back to the named IFS tables it came from — no mystery spreadsheets in the middle.
2. "It ran" isn't the same as "it's right" Checked daily
A job can finish "successfully" and still load nothing. So every morning each table is checked for three warning signs: it loaded zero rows, it lost more than 5% of its rows overnight, or it has stopped changing for over 45 days.
3. Every rule has its reason written down
Business rules aren't hidden in code. For example, internal inter-company trade is excluded so we don't double-count demand Harwin sells to itself, and figures stop at the last working day so the same report sent twice shows the same total. Each rule is recorded with why, so anyone can judge whether it still holds.
4. Checked, not assumed
Every scheduled report is confirmed by its output file or its run log — nobody assumes it landed just because it was supposed to. The flagship Bookings & Billings goes further: its totals must match a fresh calculation from the source and the loaded billings must match IFS. If they don't match, the email doesn't go.
What this means for you: if a number ever looks wrong, ask — every figure can be traced to its source. And before building your own spreadsheet from scratch, check whether it already exists: the Sales Repository has 32 ready-made reports you can export to Excel, and the data dictionary lists every table and column with what it's for. You can see pipeline health, data quality and report definitions on the Harwin Data Platform page (Harwin network only).
💡 Tips & Tricks

Six fresh ones this month, drawn straight from the stories above.

1
Give it the whole document
Claude can read very long documents in one go. For long internal documents — a quality manual, a supplier standard — share the whole thing and ask across all of it, in Claude Team, never a personal account. Customer drawings, customer specifications and anything export-controlled stay out.
2
New model, same ladder
Opus 5.5 is cheaper than before, but it's still the premium option. Start on Sonnet 5 and only step up to Opus 5.5 when the answer genuinely falls short. The same goes for the Effort setting.
3
Time your worst report
Before you nominate it for Report Rescue, note how long it actually takes from start to finish. That number is what tells us whether automation is worth it — and it makes the before-and-after story.
4
Paste the minimum
Need AI to help with a spreadsheet? Share the columns and a handful of example rows, not the whole export. You'll usually get a better answer, and far less data leaves its home.
5
Write for machines too
Anything customer-facing — datasheets, product pages, specs — is increasingly read by AI before a person. Clear headings, consistent units and complete specs help both.
6
Ask for the decisions, not just a summary
A plain summary is useful. A summary that pulls out deadlines and flags what needs your decision is far more useful. Try the prompt below on your next long document.
Try this prompt
Summarise this [DOCUMENT TYPE] in 5 bullet points for a [YOUR ROLE] at an electronic connector manufacturer. Then list any dates or deadlines it mentions, and flag anything that needs a decision from me.

Approved AI Tools at Harwin — and why it matters

A short refresher (worth repeating every month):

  • ✅ ChatGPT Enterprise — approved for all work data including PII, with appropriate redaction
  • ✅ Microsoft Copilot (in M365) — approved; lives inside your Harwin tenant boundary
  • ✅ Claude Team / Enterprise — approved; ideal for long-context / document-heavy work
  • ⚠️ Anything else (Gemini, Perplexity, local LLMs, browser-extension AIs) — check with IT first (log a ticket: itsupport@harwin.com)
  • ❌ Personal Claude Pro / ChatGPT Plus / free Copilot — not approved for any Harwin data, no matter how small the task
  • ❌ Customer drawings, customer specifications and export-controlled data — keep out of all AI tools, including approved ones, unless IT has specifically approved it
