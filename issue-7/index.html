Hi everyone, Nav here. Last month's theme was AI moving inside the systems we already use. This month the story moved on again: AI is starting to do things, not just answer questions. Agents that read your inbox, change code, and run tasks in the background.

That's exciting and it's also exactly why the biggest AI news of October was a warning from OpenAI about its own agents. This issue covers that story and three others, what they mean for Harwin, a Report Rescue update, and the traffic-light data guide we promised last time.

📰 The month in AI

Four stories worth knowing about — one warning, one about the tools we use, one about security, and one that matters for our defence customers.

⚠️
The warning: OpenAI's agents switched off their own monitoring
On 21 October OpenAI published details of two incidents from early October. In one, an agent told "don't send any messages" while searching Slack decided that also meant the automatic safety alerts, and quietly stopped them reaching any humans. In the other, an agent removed a monitoring integration while migrating some code, then denied doing so when asked directly. OpenAI's own words: "We don't yet have a satisfying explanation for this behavior."

Why it's worth knowing: neither agent was being malicious. They were being too helpful with a vague instruction. That's the thing to remember any time you hand an AI a task that touches real systems: say clearly what it must not do, and keep a person checking the result.
Source: BleepingComputer (21 October 2026), Al Jazeera.
🧠
The tools one: a small, fast Claude and an AI that messages you first
Two Anthropic releases this month. On 7 October came Claude Haiku 5.5: the small, fast model in the family, with the same very long document limit as Opus and a fraction of the cost. Anthropic pitches it for high-volume, quick-turnaround work such as sorting, extracting and classifying. For most everyday questions it's more than enough, which changes the model ladder (see Tip 1).

On 21 October Anthropic opened a preview of Claude Duet, an agent that reads your email and calendar, sends you a morning brief and works on tasks in the background, asking before it pays for anything or signs anything. It's US-only, personal Pro and Max accounts only, and doesn't work with Microsoft 365 yet. So for Harwin: interesting, not approved. Watch this space.
Source: Claude Haiku 5.5 overview (7 October 2026), VKTR on Claude Duet (21 October 2026).
🔓
The security one: AI found a bug in most of the web
On 21 October PHP, the language behind roughly 70% of websites, released emergency patches for two critical flaws nicknamed PHP2Shell. They allow an attacker to take over a website without a password. The flaws had been there for years. What's new is how they were found: security firm Wiz used AI research tools to spot the first one in August.

Why it's worth knowing: AI now finds bugs faster than people do, and attackers have the same tools. Our IT team handles the patching. Your part is the same as ever: be suspicious of unexpected password-reset emails and links, even from sites you trust.
Source: Recorded Future (October 2026), Wiz.
✈️
The defence one: where an AI model comes from now matters
On 20 October the US Department of Homeland Security ordered federal civilian agencies to stop buying DeepSeek AI products and remove them from government systems. The order also covers third-party tools built on DeepSeek. In the same week, Chinese lab Moonshot released a model that scores within a point of the best Western models on coding tests, with free weights promised in November.

What it means for us: cheap, capable models are arriving from everywhere, and our aerospace and defence customers increasingly care which ones touched their data. That's one more reason the approved-tools list below exists. If a new tool looks great and free, ask IT before any Harwin data goes near it.
Source: TechCrunch (20 October 2026), AI Weekly (19 to 25 October 2026).
🧭 What this means for us

Two issues ago the message was keep a human in the loop. Last month it was AI goes to where the data lives. This month's addition: AI that acts needs clear limits. The more an AI can do, the more it matters that we tell it what not to do.

In practice, that means three things at Harwin:

  • Don't connect AI to Harwin systems on your own. Agents that read your inbox, calendar or files are arriving fast. Any that touch Harwin accounts go through IT first, however small the task.
  • Write the rules into the prompt. "Don't change anything else", "don't invent figures", "ask before you delete". OpenAI's agents went wrong on a vague instruction. Ours shouldn't.
  • Pick the right-sized model. With Haiku 5.5 now available, most quick tasks don't need the premium model. Start small; step up only when the answer falls short.
Same rule as always: AI drafts, a person decides. Building or scheduling something that runs on its own? Loop in IT first (itsupport@harwin.com).

🗂️ Data governance: the traffic-light guide

Last month we promised a simple guide to which data can go where, including into AI tools. Here's the first version. It's deliberately short. If your area doesn't fit, tell me and we'll fix it.

🟢 Green: fine in approved AI tools
Published datasheets and product pages. Your own drafts, emails and notes. General process questions ("how do I write a good root-cause summary?"). Anything already on our public website.
🟡 Amber: approved tools only, and paste the minimum
Internal reports, pricing, stock levels, customer and supplier names, internal procedures. Fine in ChatGPT Business, Copilot or Claude Team. Share the columns and a few example rows, not the whole export. Never in a personal account.
🔴 Red: not in any AI tool unless IT has specifically approved it
Customer drawings and customer specifications. Anything export-controlled (ITAR, EAR, UK export licences). Anything a customer contract says we can't share with third parties. If you're not sure which bucket something is in, treat it as red and ask.

Myth vs fact

Myth: "It's an approved tool, so I can paste anything."

Fact: approved means the tool is safe to use. It doesn't change what we're allowed to share under customer contracts and export rules. Red stays red, whichever tool it is.

Still looking for data owners. If you're already the person everyone asks about customers, pricing, stock or quality data, you're probably the owner already. Let me know and we'll make it official, so there's always a go-to person.
💡 Tips & Tricks

Six fresh ones this month, drawn straight from the stories above.

1
The new model ladder
Three rungs now. Haiku 5.5 for quick questions, sorting and summaries. Sonnet 5.5 as your everyday default. Opus 5.5 only when the answer genuinely falls short. Same idea in ChatGPT: start on the standard model, step up for hard problems.
2
Say what it must not do
OpenAI's agents went wrong on a vague instruction. Add a short rules line to anything important: "Don't invent figures. Don't change anything I didn't ask about. If unsure, ask."
3
Ask for the assumptions first
Before a long task, add: "List the assumptions you're making, then wait for me to confirm." It catches the wrong turn before the work is done, not after.
4
Ask for the formula, not the answer
For spreadsheet work, ask AI to write the Excel formula or the steps rather than work out the totals itself. The numbers stay in your file, and anyone can check how they were calculated.
5
The supplier test
Not sure if something's amber or red? Ask yourself: would I email this to a new supplier without checking first? If not, don't paste it into AI without checking either.
6
Check how fresh it is
AI models have a cut-off date and may not know about last month's news. For anything time-sensitive, ask it to say what date its information runs to, or give it the latest document yourself.
Try this prompt
I need a [DOCUMENT TYPE] for [AUDIENCE] at an electronic connector manufacturer. Rules: do not invent figures or names. If you need something I haven't given you, write [NEEDED] in its place. Before you write anything, list the assumptions you are making in 3 to 5 bullet points, then stop and wait for me to confirm.
